The forest had always been strange after midnight.

The trees stood like sentinels in the dark, their bark grey as bone in the silver moonlight. No wind moved through the branches, yet the leaves trembled.

Maria had come here a dozen times before — always in daylight, always with others. Tonight she was alone. The call she'd received that afternoon had left her no choice. A voice she hadn't heard in eleven years. A voice that belonged to someone who was supposed to be dead.

She pressed deeper into the tree line, her torch throwing a narrow cone of yellow light ahead of her. Somewhere in the distance, water moved over stone. The old creek. She was heading in the right direction.

A branch snapped behind her. She froze.

The silence stretched like a held breath. Then — nothing. Just the slow tick of cooling bark and the faraway hoot of an owl. She exhaled, steadied herself, and walked on.

The clearing appeared between two ancient oaks, exactly as the voice had described. In its centre stood a structure she had never noticed before: a small stone altar, draped in dark moss, with a single lantern burning on top. The flame was steady, undisturbed by any breeze.

She stepped into the clearing. The lantern light caught the edges of something resting on the altar. A photograph.

Her hand trembled as she picked it up.

It was her. Standing right here. In this clearing. But the date printed on the back of the photograph was tomorrow.
