# Shot Rules — Horror Preset
# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# Horror-specific camera and shot composition rules for StoryFrame Studio.
# Loaded automatically when this file is present alongside images.txt in the
# same preset folder.
# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CAMERA STYLE

cinematic film still
35mm film look with subtle grain
wide or medium shots — DO NOT use close-up portrait
shallow depth of field — sharp foreground subject, soft ominous background
subject or key element slightly off-centre for unease
low angles to make environments feel towering and oppressive
eye-level shots down corridors, paths, or tunnels to emphasise depth and
darkness

Scenes must work well for slow Ken Burns zoom and pan motion.

SHOT COMPOSITION PRIORITIES (in order)

1. Establish the environment fully before introducing any character or object.
2. When an object is the focus, show it within its surroundings — not isolated.
3. When a character is present, frame them small within a large, threatening
   space.
4. Use depth: foreground element, mid-ground subject, ominous background.
5. Leave significant empty or dark space in the frame — the unseen is more
   frightening than the seen.
6. Avoid symmetry; slightly unbalanced compositions create subliminal unease.
7. Doorways, windows, and thresholds should frame darkness or uncertainty
   beyond them.
