# Consistency Anchors — Horror Preset
# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# Horror-specific visual anchors for StoryFrame Studio.
# Injected into every image prompt to maintain a consistent horror atmosphere
# and visual language across all generated scenes, regardless of the specific
# story being told.
# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

# --- Atmosphere ---
Every scene must convey a pervasive sense of unease and dread. Even calm or
quiet moments should feel like something is about to go wrong. The environment
itself should feel hostile, watchful, or suffocating — as if the space does
not want the characters there.

# --- Lighting consistency ---
Maintain a consistent low-key lighting approach throughout the story. Dominant
shadows, limited light sources, and large areas of near-black. Light should
feel fragile and insufficient — small pools surrounded by overwhelming
darkness. When transitioning between scenes, lighting mood should remain
cohesive unless the narration explicitly shifts setting or time of day.

# --- Color grading ---
Apply a consistent cold, desaturated color grade across all scenes. Deep
blacks, steely blues, muted greens, and ashen grays form the base palette.
Warm tones appear only from diegetic sources (fire, old bulbs, candles) and
should feel isolated against the cold surroundings. This color language must
remain uniform across the entire story.

# --- Environmental texture ---
Surfaces and environments should show texture and imperfection: grain in wood,
cracks in walls, moisture on stone, dust in the air, rust on metal. These
details ground the horror in physical reality and make spaces feel lived-in,
aged, or decaying. Maintain the same level of textural detail and wear across
all scenes set in the same location.

# --- Character depiction ---
When characters appear, show them as mid-distance or partial figures — seen
from behind, standing in doorways, as silhouettes against dim light, or
partially obscured by environment. Never show a clear, well-lit face. Convey
emotion through body language, posture, and their small scale within the
larger, threatening environment. Maintain consistent depiction of recurring
characters (clothing, build, silhouette shape) across scenes.

# --- Compositional tension ---
Use off-centre framing, negative space, and depth to create visual tension.
Leave dark, empty areas in the frame that suggest unseen presences. Doorways,
corridors, and openings should recede into blackness. The viewer's eye should
be drawn to a focal point while remaining aware of the threatening void around
it. This compositional approach must be consistent across all scenes.

# --- Scale and isolation ---
Characters should appear small relative to their environment whenever possible.
Large empty rooms, vast dark landscapes, towering structures, and long
corridors all serve to emphasize human vulnerability. Even in confined spaces,
use angles that make the walls feel like they are closing in. The sense of
isolation or entrapment should persist across scenes.
